# B-65 Device R3 levers: lock, retire, wipe and isolate with dual approval

Status: **mocked** (six PowerShell levers over an offline mock device state, registry fragment, notice templates, tests). The approval rules are the runner's own (B-28, B-42); this item registers levers that use them. No real RMM, Intune or Defender is called. Synthetic data only.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory: `W-EP-08` (lost or stolen device), `W-ONB-12` (offboarding: retire or wipe), `W-SEC-05` (endpoint isolation). Controls: `C-05`, `C-06`, `C-07`, `C-29`, `C-30`, `C-32`. Depends on `B-61` (RMM and Intune mock adapters; device ids and owners come from their fixtures), `B-28` (R3 dual approval, 24-hour freshness), `B-16` (PowerShell bridge), `B-42` (windows, caps, pre-authorized containment). Standards: `STD-LV`, `STD-PS`, `STD-TS`. Decisions in force: D6-05, D6-06, D6-08, HO-3, HO-6.

## Why

A stolen laptop is contained fast, and nobody wipes the wrong device. Every lever here changes one device, needs two leads, and shows the blast radius before anything is applied.

## Levers (`levers/registry.d/device-r3.json`, module `levers/powershell/DeviceR3/`)

| Lever | Risk | Reversibility | Undo | Verification | Window | Pre-authorized containment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `device.lock` | R3 | reversible | `device.unlock` | requester+contact | business-hours | yes |
| `device.unlock` | R2 | reversible | undo of lock | requester | none | no |
| `device.isolate` | R3 | reversible | `device.release` | requester | business-hours | yes |
| `device.release` | R3 | reversible | undo of isolate | requester | none | no |
| `device.retire` | R3 | **irreversible** | none | requester+contact | business-hours | **no** |
| `device.wipe` | R3 | **irreversible** | none | requester+contact | business-hours | **no** |

- Params on all six: `device` (an RMM device uid, one per call) and `upn` (the device's assigned user). The lever refuses a `upn` that is not the device's recorded owner, so a mistyped device id cannot land on someone else's machine. `upn` also lets the notice template and the runner's privileged-account check work.
- **Retire is irreversible** here (decision, see the change note): removing company data and management cannot be put back, only re-enrolled. The roadmap names wipe as irreversible and says retire never takes pre-authorized containment; marking retire irreversible makes the runner enforce both.
- `device.wipe` only moves the device to `wipe.state: requested`. Starting the wipe in the RMM or Intune console is a person's job after approval (D6-08); nothing in the repo starts it. Wipe and retire are refused for a device under `legalHold`, and for one already retired.
- Every lever is convergent: the same call twice is an empty diff.
- Dry-run `data` is the blast radius: devices affected (always 1), device and owner, online or offline (an offline device acts on its next check-in), whether it is the client's only device for a role, regulated data, legal hold, share of the client's devices, and a plain `undo` line.

## Acceptance

- **B65-1** lock, retire, wipe and Defender isolate are R3: two leads approve, the requester is verified, one device per approval
- **B65-2** wipe is `irreversible`: approval older than 24 hours is refused by the runner rule from B28-5 and the page shows `no undo`; isolate has `release` as its undo
- **B65-3** after-hours execution is refused unless the client record allows it, enforced by runner rules B42-4 and B42-5; wipe and retire never use pre-authorized containment, and wipe stays human-executed after approval (D6-08)

Tests: `tests/py/devices/test_b65_device_r3.py` (`uv run --frozen pytest -p no:cacheprovider tests/py/devices/test_b65_device_r3.py`), functions named `test_b65_<k>_...`, including a fooled-model test: a scripted model that has read an injected ticket tries every shortcut, and the runner denies each. PowerShell: `levers/powershell/DeviceR3/tests/DeviceR3.Tests.ps1`.

## Rollback

`device.lock` and `device.isolate` name an undo lever, so `workflows.rollback` proposes `device.unlock` or `device.release` from the audit record (a lead still approves it). Wipe and retire have none: the rollback workflow returns the manual steps from the module README and proposes nothing. A test asserts that outcome and that no undo lever can be invoked.

## Known limits

- The approval queue and page (`core/queue/store.py`, `ui/app.py`) still send one approval, and they run a runner without a policy. Under a policy-bearing runner a single page approval is denied, which fails closed, but the page cannot yet collect the second lead. That wiring is not in this item's owned files.
- A named `window_override` on an approval lifts the window for any lever, including wipe and retire (runner rule B42-4, not changed here). The audit keeps the override name. A lever-level "never override" flag is proposed, not built.
- `requester+contact` refuses a device whose owner holds a directory role (D6-06), so a lost admin laptop needs the human procedure. Isolate and release use `requester` only.
- Notice drafting (B-04) is unchanged and needs a `upn`, which every lever here has.
- Cross-process races, real RMM behaviour and field names are not modelled; see `levers/powershell/DeviceR3/SOURCES.md` (all UNVERIFIED).
