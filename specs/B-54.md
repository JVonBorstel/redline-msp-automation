# B-54 Expiry watch: DMARC/SPF/DKIM, app secrets, TLS certificates, GDAP relationships

Status: **built** (workflow, CLI, fixtures, tests). Report only. Synthetic data only.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory (impact chains): `W-M365-10` (DMARC, SPF, DKIM), `W-IDM-12` (app secret expiry), `W-VND-05` (TLS expiry), `W-IDM-10` (GDAP). Controls: `C-38` (delegated access is least privilege and time-bound), `C-20`, `C-21`. Depends on: nothing. Standards: `STD-FX`, `STD-TS`. Decision in force: D6-09 (GDAP, no Global Admin).

## Why

Mail stops being spoofable and nothing expires by surprise on a Monday. All four jobs are reading a date or a policy and telling a named person in time. None needs a write, so none gets one: a renewal, a DNS change or a new relationship is a person's job (HO-1; W-VND-06 for DNS).

## Behaviour

`python -m workflows.expiry <client> [--as-of <date>] [--within-days N] [--markdown]` reads `fixtures/dns/<client>.json`, `fixtures/dns/<client>-dmarc-<domain>.xml` and `fixtures/partner/<client>.json` and prints reminder rows.

- **Domains.** SPF (one record; `-all` pass, `~all` warn, otherwise fail; more than 10 lookups fail), DKIM (selectors listed and published), DMARC (`p=reject|quarantine` with `rua` and `pct=100` pass; `p=none`, missing `rua` or `pct<100` warn; missing or invalid fail). Record text only; no DNS lookup.
- **Expiry.** App secrets, app certificates, TLS certificates and GDAP relationships that end within N days (default 30; GDAP at least 60) or have ended. A GDAP relationship that holds the Global Administrator role is listed whatever its date.
- **DMARC aggregate.** `parse_dmarc_aggregate` reads an RFC 7489 Appendix C style report (the format a collector such as EasyDMARC, MP-22, receives) and gives per domain: policy, alignment modes, messages, DMARC pass and fail (pass = aligned DKIM or aligned SPF), per-mechanism pass counts and the failing sources. A document with a DTD or entity is refused.
- **Reminder row.** `client, kind, subject, status, due, owner, detail, days_left, action`. `due` is the expiry date, or the as-of date for a policy gap. `owner` comes from the fixture; an item without one shows `unassigned` (the missing owner is the thing to fix). `action` is always `reminder-only`.
- **Isolation.** The client is explicit; only that client's files are opened; a file whose `client` differs from its name is refused; an aggregate report for a domain the client does not own is refused.
- **Secrets.** Credentials are metadata (id, label, end date). A fixture field that could hold a secret value (`secretText`, `secret`, `value`, `password`, `privateKey`, `key`) is refused at load.

## Acceptance

- **B54-1** DNS fixtures (no live lookups in tests) are checked for SPF, DKIM and DMARC policy per domain; app-registration secrets and certificates expiring within N days and GDAP relationships expiring or holding Global Administrator are listed per client.
- **B54-2** Fixture shapes for Partner Center GDAP are marked UNVERIFIED field by field (`fixtures/partner/SOURCES.md`; a test checks every fixture field appears there).
- **B54-3** Every reminder is a report row with the date and owner; nothing is renewed by automation (the module has no write path and imports no lever runner).
- **B54-4** DMARC aggregate reports are read from a fixture shaped like the RFC 7489 aggregate XML that EasyDMARC collects (MP-22; cited); pass, fail, policy and alignment are shown per domain.

Tests: `tests/py/expiry/test_b54_expiry.py` (run with `uv run --frozen pytest -p no:cacheprovider tests/py/expiry`).

## Known limits

- Every vendor field name (Partner Center GDAP, Graph application, RFC element list as typed) is UNVERIFIED: written from memory, no vendor page was opened. The Global Administrator role id is UNVERIFIED too.
- Fixtures are synthetic and hand-written with the checks in view; a pass shows the pipeline works, not that real records look like this.
- DNS records are read as text. Real lookups, 10-lookup counting through nested includes, DKIM key strength and certificate chain checks are not done.
- Owner names are the synthetic identities; a real owner map is the PSA/CMDB adapter's job.
