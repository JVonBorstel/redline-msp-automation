# B-60 Answer drafts: security questionnaires, cyber insurance, proposals

Status: **built** (workflow, CLI, tests). Offline: no model is called, no network. Synthetic questionnaires only. Every output is a DRAFT for a named person to review; nothing is approved, sent or submitted.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory: `W-CMP-09` (security questionnaires), `W-CMP-08` (cyber insurance), `W-PRJ-06` (project scoping, proposal sections). Controls: `C-26` (per-client retrieval isolation), `C-45` (synthetic-only data), `C-41` (evaluation before trust: the claim-inflation tests). Depends on `B-57` (cited retrieval). Standards: `STD-DC`, `STD-TS`.

## Why

Hours of copy-paste per questionnaire become a review of cited drafts. The risk is the opposite of slow: a fluent draft that says more than anyone can show ("we are SOC 2 certified", "MFA is enforced in production"). So the workflow drafts only what its evidence says, in wording that keeps the evidence's strength, and hands everything else to a person.

## Behaviour

Package `workflows/questionnaires/` (`drafts.py`, `__main__.py`, `__init__.py`). Fixtures: `fixtures/questionnaires/` (see `SOURCES.md` there).

- **Input.** A questionnaire file with `"synthetic": true`, one client id, a kind (`security`, `insurance`, `proposal`) and items. Each item has a `scope`: `msp` (answer from MSP policy passages), `client` (answer from that client's own records), `scoping` (answer from past projects, with a `project_kind`). A file not marked synthetic, with an unusable client id, an unknown key or a repeated id is refused. The owner (`--owner`) is required and must be a name: blank, one-letter, number-only and placeholders (`tbd`, `owner`, `n/a`, `unknown` ...) are refused.
- **Three kinds of evidence, nothing else.** (1) A sentence quoted word for word from a retrieved passage (B-57 `candidates`, same threshold, tripwire re-scan), cited by chunk id and article id. (2) A repo control from `map/controls.json` that is built or partial **and** has at least one test in `governance/soc2_mapping.json` that exists in the repo; cited by control id and test id, with its status and its "not demonstrated" lines. A planned control, or one with no existing test, is never cited. (3) For scoping, the actual hours of similar past synthetic projects as a range, citing each project id.
- **Fixed wording.** Every claim is a fixed lead plus the evidence. The lead says what kind of statement follows: `MSP policy KB-POL-001 "..." states:`, `Client record KB-CQ-001 "..." states:`, `Repo control C-08 (...), status built, is described as:`, `Past synthetic projects of kind ... logged actual hours`. The workflow never writes "does", "is enforced", "is certified" or "is compliant". Every answered draft carries the limit: it is not evidence that anything operates, is enforced, or has been tested in a real environment.
- **B60-1 refusal ("needs a person").** A question is refused, with a reason, when it asks for certification, attestation or framework status (SOC 2, ISO, HIPAA compliant ...); for a result or count from real operation (how many incidents, percentages, "have you had ..."); whether something is enforced, deployed, currently running or in production; when no passage reaches the threshold and no usable control matches; and when the question text trips the injection tripwire (the text is not echoed: it is replaced by a withheld notice). The roadmap's `needs a human answer` and the task's `needs a person` are the same marker; the rendering carries both.
- **B60-2 scopes.** `msp` items quote only `msp-internal` passages. `client` items quote only passages from the caller's own namespace; MSP policy alone never answers a client fact and another client's records are never retrieved (the store is asked for exactly `[client:<id>, msp-internal]`, B-57). Control claims are added to `msp` items only. Answers about controls cite the control id.
- **B60-3 scoping.** Projects of the same kind visible to the client (its own and `msp-internal`; another client's are invisible) minus any whose title or note trips the tripwire. With at least three: `between <min> and <max> hours across <n> projects.` plus every project id. Never a mean, a median or a single number; the text says it is not an estimate, quote or promise. Fewer than three: `needs a person`.
- **Claim inflation.** `check_draft(ctx, client, draft)` re-derives every claim from its own citations and reports any difference: lead, quote, control objective, range, scope, a citation the caller may not read, a planned control, a test that does not exist, a refusal that carries a claim. `inflation_problems(claim, evidence)` is the independent word check: a strong word (`always`, `never`, `all`, `every`, `enforced`, `verified`, `production`, `certified`, `compliant`, `audited`, `guarantee` ...) or a number in a claim body must also appear in its evidence. The CLI runs `check_draft` on every draft and refuses to write a file if one fails.
- **CLI.** `uv run --frozen python -m workflows.questionnaires draft <file> --owner "<name>" [--out DIR]` writes `<name>.md` and `<name>.json` (status `DRAFT`, `requires_review_by`, per item `DRAFT` or `needs a person`) or prints markdown. Exit 0 drafted, 2 refused input. Dates are fixed (`AS_OF`), the clock is never read.
- **Injection guard.** The workflow imports `detect_injection` (question text, project text); `tests/py/evals/test_b79_injection_v2.py` lists `questionnaires` under `GUARDED_NOT_EXERCISED` (the B-79 corpus does not run it). Ingestion (B-56) withholds a whole chunk that trips a rule, so the clean sentence beside a planted one is withheld too.

## Acceptance

- **B60-1** each answer cites MSP-internal policy passages; a question without a source is marked `needs a human answer`
- **B60-2** client-specific facts come only from that client's namespace; answers about controls cite the control id
- **B60-3** project scoping drafts list similar past synthetic projects with their actual hours as cited ranges, never a single promised number

Tests: `tests/py/questionnaires/test_b60_questionnaires.py`, functions named `test_b60_<k>_...`; written before the code (first run: ImportError on the missing package).

## Interpretation notes

- **B60-1, "cites MSP-internal policy passages".** Policy items cite them. Where the question maps to a repo control, the draft also (or instead) cites the control id and its tests, as the task allows ("a repo control/test"). An item that only a client record can answer (`client` scope) cites that record, not policy.
- **Present-tense questions.** "Are backup copies encrypted?" is answered with the policy sentence, led by "MSP policy ... states". The reviewer decides whether that is enough; the draft never turns it into "yes".
- **Crosswalk.** Which controls back which question words is `fixtures/questionnaires/crosswalk.json`, a reviewer's mapping. A wrong row can attach an irrelevant (but true and labelled) control; it cannot make a claim stronger.

## Known limits (all UNVERIFIED against real use)

- UNVERIFIED: nothing was run with a real questionnaire, insurer form, proposal or model. The three questionnaires, the policy sentences, the client records and the project hours are invented. Fixture questions were worded so the word-overlap `HashEmbedder` finds them (threshold 0.38 from B-57); a paraphrase with no shared words is refused. A real embedder needs the B-57 eval re-run.
- The refusal word lists (certification, result, production) are keyword rules written by one author. A question worded around them ("Is MFA a hard requirement everywhere?") is not caught and is answered with a policy quote that stays a policy quote; that is the design's second line, not a guarantee. The wording is checked on fixtures, not on real forms.
- A real policy sentence can itself overclaim ("All admins use MFA"). The draft would quote it, led by "states". Garbage policy in, quoted garbage out; the reviewer is the control.
- Test ids come from the B-95 mapping; this workflow checks they exist, not that they pass (the suite does that, on fixtures). The controls are repo controls on synthetic fixtures; none is shown running anywhere real.
- The range counts projects of one `kind` string; "similar" is not sized, scoped or dated. Hours are invented. At least three projects is a choice, not a statistical rule.
- No Excel, PDF or portal export; no approval flow (a DRAFT stays a DRAFT until a person copies it somewhere else); the named owner is a string, not an identity check.
- Injection detection is the tripwire (D-019), not the control; the control is that nothing here acts on text and every claim is a checked quote.
