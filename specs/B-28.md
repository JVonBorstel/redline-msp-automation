# B-28 Runner: idempotency enforcement, per-client lock, audit v2 fields, R3 dual approval

Status: **built** (`core/levers/runner.py`, `core/levers/models.py`, `tests/py/levers/test_b28_runner3.py`). Approver sign-in is checked against the **mock** B-89 issuer; nothing here has run against a real Entra tenant.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory: `W-TOOL-01`, `W-ONB-02`, `W-EP-08`. Controls: `C-18`, `C-29`, `C-06`, `C-07`, `C-08`. Depends on `B-16`, `B-17`, `B-89`. Standards: `STD-LV` (STD-LV-4, STD-LV-6a), `STD-AU`, `STD-PY`, `STD-TS`. Decisions in force: D-024, D6-05, D6-06, D6-07.

## Why

The rules that identity changes at scale depend on live in the runner, so a new lever family (devices, mailboxes) inherits them without editing `runner.py`: who may approve, how many, how fresh, how they proved who they are, and that one approved change is not applied twice by a different approval.

## Design

`LeverRunner(..., policy: RunnerPolicy | None = None, clock=None)`.

- **Always on** (needs no policy): one execute lock per client (a dict of locks), and `request_id`, `actor_kind` (`agent` for an agent identity, `human` for any other known one, `unknown` for an unknown identity) and `lever_version` (null for an unregistered lever) on every record the runner writes, denials and pause records included.
- **With a `RunnerPolicy`** (the B-28 approval rules). Without one the runner keeps the W1 behaviour (one lead approval, no sign-in proof); the offline CLIs still run that way. `python -m ui` always passes a policy (see "Dual approval in the queue and the page" below).
  - `invoke(..., approval=)` takes one `Approval` or a list. Each must name the same lever, client and params, come from a lead scoped to the client, and not be the requester. Two approvals from one lead count once and are refused.
  - Minimum distinct approvers: R1 and R2 one, R3 two; `LeverDef.min_approvers` and the client record's `approver_minimum` can raise it. The effective minimum is the largest of the three.
  - R2 and R3 approvals must be signed in: `Approval.token` (an Entra ID token) is validated by the policy's `TokenValidator` at the runner's clock and must name the approver; or `Approval.demo` when `RunnerPolicy(demo=True)`. The audit result lists each approver with `auth` (`entra-id-token` or `demo-picker`), `demo` and `sid`. The token is excluded from the model's dump and repr and is never written.
  - An `irreversible` lever needs every approval to carry `approved_at` less than 24 hours old (not in the future). `undo_notice(spec)` returns `no undo`; the approval page (`ui/app.py`, outside this item) already prints `NO_UNDO`.
  - Replay guard (STD-LV-6a): before an execute of a write lever, the audit log is read; a record with the same idempotency key and `applied: true` whose `approval_id` differs from this approval's is a denial. `approval_id` is `appr-` plus 24 hex of SHA-256 over lever, client, params and the sorted approvers. The same approval presented again is allowed (the handler converges to "no change").
  - `verification: "requester+contact"`: a `callback-on-file` record whose `ref` is `number-on-file:<source>` (source in the page's list, the number never recorded), and an `Approval.contact_verification` (`ContactConfirmation`: `by`, `at`, `contact` record id) whose `contact` is in the client record's `authorized_contacts`. Both recorders must be humans scoped to the client. A target whose `upn` appears in the tenant state's `directoryRoles[].members[]` is refused (human-only procedure).

## Acceptance

- **B28-1** a second execute whose idempotency key already has an `applied: true` record not authorised by this approval is denied and audited (STD-LV-6a)
- **B28-2** executes for two different clients run concurrently; two for one client serialise (SL-05); a test proves both
- **B28-3** an R3 lever executes only with two approvals from two different leads, neither the requester, both scoped to the client (HO-3); a single approval is denied and audited
- **B28-4** every record the runner writes carries request_id, actor_kind and lever_version
- **B28-5** an `irreversible` lever executes only with an approval younger than 24 hours, and the approval page shows `no undo` (STD-LV-4)
- **B28-6** an R2 or R3 approval is accepted only from an approver authenticated by the B-89 token validator; the demo picker works only behind `--demo` and its approvals are marked `demo` in the audit (D6-05, D-024)
- **B28-7** the approver minimum per risk class can be raised per lever or per client from the client record, never lowered; a lowered value fails the load (D6-05)
- **B28-8** `verification: "requester+contact"` needs callback-on-file (source recorded, never the number) and confirmation by the client's authorized contact; a privileged target is refused (D6-06)

## Dual approval in the queue and the page (follow-up, D-067)

Change note: `docs/changes/B-28-dual-approval.md`. `python -m ui` runs the runner under a policy (`--demo` is `RunnerPolicy(demo=True)`, `--auth-config` the token validator, `--client-records FILE` optional). For a lever that needs more than one lead, the queue keeps the first approval as `awaiting-second-approval` (verification, sign-in record, the runner's attestation; the token is never stored) and executes only when a different lead approves.

- **B28D-1** the first lead's approval is checked by the runner (lead, scope, four-eyes, sign-in, requester verification) and recorded as a partial approval; nothing is applied; the partial is audited with the approver's sign-in record
- **B28D-2** a second, different lead completes it; only then does the runner execute, once, with both approvals, and the execute record names both with how each signed in
- **B28D-3** refused and audited: the same lead twice, the requester, a lead outside the client, a first approval at or past the age limit (24 hours) or from another runner process, an approval of a proposal whose content changed, and a stale page (digest)
- **B28D-4** rejection by either lead closes it; nothing is applied
- **B28D-5** a lever that needs one lead, and a queue whose runner has no policy, keep the single-approval path
- **B28D-6** a `requester+contact` lever collects a callback on the number on file and one of the client record's authorized contacts before the first approval is recorded; the page offers the contacts from the record only
- **B28D-7** the page shows `Approved by X, waiting for a second lead`, gives the first lead no second Approve, and lets a different signed-in lead (token, or the `--demo` picker, which is not authentication) complete it; an end-to-end test approves `user.reset-mfa` and `device.lock` as two leads and verifies the chain

Tests: `tests/py/queue/test_b28_queue_dual.py`, `tests/py/ui/test_b28_page_dual.py`, `tests/py/ui/test_b28_dual_e2e.py`.

## Limits and open points

- Wired for the page only. `python -m ui` runs under a policy; the offline CLIs (`python -m core.levers invoke --execute --approved-by`, the workflow CLIs and `tools/packaged_demo.py`, which call `open_queue()` without one) still execute an R3 lever on one approval with no sign-in check (W1).
- A partial approval does not survive a restart of the page: the attestation key is in memory only, so the next approval is refused, audited, and the proposal returns to `pending` (fail closed, D-067).
- The replay guard is literal: a legitimate repeat of the same change later (for example disabling the same user again after re-enabling) needs a new idempotency key or a deliberate carve-out. Proposed in the change note.
- The audit log is scanned per execute (O(n) in log size); an index is a later scaling item.
- The privileged-role check reads `directoryRoles` from the tenant state. The W2 mock tenant files have none; a real check needs the Graph adapter (B-19 shape `directoryRoles?$expand=members`, UNVERIFIED against a live tenant). Any directory role counts as privileged.
- Cross-process execution (two runner processes on one state) is still not locked; the replay guard is the only cross-process defence.
