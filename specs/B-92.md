# B-92 AI policy, acceptable use and DLP mapping

Status: **document-only** (three governance pages and the test that keeps them true to the map and the code). Synthetic data only. Not legal advice.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory: `W-GOV-01`. Controls: `C-42` (staff training and acceptable use), `C-25` (data classification and pre-prompt redaction gate), `C-45` (synthetic data only and honest labels). Depends on `B-55` (the data classes and the gate in code). Standards: `STD-DC`. Decisions in force: D6-11 (hosted models off, `model_processing: none`), D6-12 (industry floors), D6-17 (roles, not names), D6-19 (consumer access decisions stay human).

## Why

One short policy staff can actually follow. The governance map defines seven data classes and B-55 enforces part of them in code, but a technician has no page that says, in plain words, what may be pasted where.

## Behaviour

- `governance/AI_POLICY.md`: scope, five rules, the seven data classes with the floor rules, what the code enforces today (paths cited), the never-automate list, hosted models and new tools, accountability and incidents.
- `governance/ACCEPTABLE_USE.md`: a class-by-destination table, do and do-not lists, and the 22 never-automate entries by id and name.
- `governance/DLP_MAPPING.md`: per class DC-0 to DC-6, what may enter a local model prompt, a hosted model prompt, the audit log and a retrieval index; the industry floor table; what the code enforces and what it does not.
- Every page starts with a status line (STD-DC-1) and the labels block (STD-DC-2).

## Acceptance

- **B92-1** the policy and acceptable-use page each fit on two pages and reference data classes DC-0 to DC-6 and the never-automate list. Two pages is measured as at most 1,200 words and at most 120 lines of Markdown source (stricter than twice B-11's 850-word page). "References the never-automate list" means the page names `governance/never-automate.json`; the acceptable-use page lists every entry id in that file, and the policy's entry count matches the file.
- **B92-2** DLP mapping lists for each data class what may enter a local prompt, a hosted prompt, a log and an index, matching governance map section 1 exactly (a test compares the tables). The local, hosted and audit-log cells are compared verbatim with `docs/map/05-governance.md` section 1. The index column is not in the map, so it is labelled as derived and every class must have a value. The floor table is compared with `INDUSTRY_FLOOR` and `DEFAULT_FLOOR` in `core/guard/classify.py`, and the hosted refusal claim with `HOSTED_REFUSED_FROM` in `core/gateway/gateway.py`.
- **B92-3** every page carries the synthetic-data and not-legal-advice labels: the exact phrases "Synthetic data only." and "Not legal advice." in the labels block within the first ten lines.

Also checked (STD-DC): every backticked repo path on the three pages exists; every `C-nn` cited is in `map/controls.json`; every `W-` id cited is in `map/inventory.json`.

Tests: `tests/py/governance/test_b92_policy.py` (`uv run --frozen pytest -p no:cacheprovider tests/py/governance -o addopts=`), functions named `test_b92_<k>_...`.

## Known limits

- The page-length check counts words and lines, not a rendered PDF.
- The index column is a proposal derived from `ISO-2`, `ARC-12` and rule 1; no retrieval index exists yet (`C-26`, Phase 5), so nothing enforces it.
- The hosted conditions for DC-1 to DC-3 and the audit-log column beyond secrets are rules for people; no code checks them (`C-28` planned).
- D6-19 (consumer access decisions) is a written rule; `governance/never-automate.json` has no pattern for it (changing that file needs a decision record and is outside this item).
