# B-52 Regulatory evidence packs per client, BAA and vendor tracking

Status: **built** (workflow, CLI, tests), on fixtures. A pack is a set of pages plus a hashed manifest. It is a mapping for the client's compliance owner. It is not legal advice, a gap assessment, a certification or a statement that any rule is met. Synthetic data only.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory: `W-CMP-02` (HIPAA security risk analysis support: technical evidence only), `W-CMP-03` (financial-client Safeguards evidence), `W-CMP-06` (BAA and vendor due-diligence tracking, read-only). Controls: `C-25` (data classes per tenant), `C-43` (vendor register), `C-45` (synthetic data, honest labels). Depends on `B-51` (manifest and audit-pinning pattern). Decision `D6-12` (regimes are set by the client's compliance owner, not by code). Standards: `STD-DC`, `STD-FX`.

## Why

Small regulated clients owe their own regulators evidence, and the MSP can supply the technical part. A dental client answers to HIPAA, a financial client to the FTC Safeguards Rule, an investment adviser also to Regulation S-P. The pack lines up what the repo can show against the items each regime names, so the client's compliance owner can check it, and it keeps a tracker of the vendors and agreements that touch the client's data.

## Behaviour

`python -m workflows.regpacks` (module `workflows/regpacks/pack.py`):

- **`build <client> --as <lead> [--as-of YYYY-MM-DD] [--write]`**. **Dry run by default**: it builds the manifest, prints its hash and file count, and writes nothing. `--write` writes `build/regpacks/<as_of>/<client>/`.
- **Regimes come from data.** `fixtures/regpacks/clients.json` stands in for the `regimes` list the compliance owner sets per client (D6-12). Contoso Dental is `hipaa`; Fabrikam Capital is `glba-ftc-safeguards` and `sec-reg-s-p`; Litware Legal has none and is refused ("no regimes set"). An unknown regime name is refused. Nothing infers a regime from a client's name or industry.
- **One page per regime** (`hipaa.md`, `glba-ftc-safeguards.md`, `sec-reg-s-p.md`), items from `fixtures/regpacks/mappings.json`:
  - `hipaa`: 45 CFR 164.308 and 164.312 items; each cites a `BA-n` row of `docs/map/05-governance.md`.
  - `glba-ftc-safeguards`: 16 CFR 314.4 elements; each cites a `GLBA-n` row.
  - `sec-reg-s-p`: incident response program, service-provider oversight, customer notice. Every line is marked `HEDGED` and the page says so at the top: the citations are from memory and are not checked against the rule text. The governance map has no S-P rows, so each line cites the nearest `GLBA-n` row. A healthcare client that is also an adviser gets both pages.
- **Mapping lines.** Each line has the item, citation, governance-map id, repo controls, and the client's synthetic findings (`fixtures/regpacks/findings.json`) whose topics overlap the item. An item with no finding reads "no finding supplied (not evidence that the item is met)". A finding that matches no item is listed in `mapping.json` under `unmapped_findings` and as a manifest gap. Every governance id must exist in `docs/map/05-governance.md` and every control id in `map/controls.json`, or the build is refused.
- **Tracker** (`vendors.md`, `vendors.json`): each vendor row has id, name, data classes, contract status, BAA status, last review date, next review due, a review state (`current`, `due-soon` within 60 days, `overdue`) worked out from `as_of`, and flags (`contract-expired`, `contract-unsigned`, `baa-not-signed` for a HIPAA client). Rows are `fixtures/regpacks/vendors.json` (invented vendors). A financial client's tracker cites `GLBA-3` (service-provider oversight), a HIPAA client's `BA-1` and `BA-3`; both cite `C-43`. It is read-only: nothing is chased, signed or sent.
- **Labels.** Every Markdown page carries "Mapping for the client's compliance owner; not legal advice. Fixture-based (synthetic data); not a certification." under its title and again as its last line. The manifest lists the labels, the data class (DC-2) and an `unverified` list.
- **Manifest** (`manifest.json` plus `manifest.json.sha256`, `sha256sum` format): files with path, size and SHA-256, regimes, labels, gaps, unverified list. Canonical JSON as in B-49 and B-51. No wall-clock time is in any file; the same inputs and `as_of` give the same bytes.
- **`verify <pack dir> [--audit-dir <dir>]`**: re-hashes the manifest against its sidecar and every file against the manifest, and fails on an edited, missing or unlisted file. With `--audit-dir`, the manifest hash must be pinned by a successful `regpack.build` execute record in the client's chain, and that chain must verify. Exit 0 match, 1 mismatch, 2 bad input or refused.
- **Who and audit.** Only a lead scoped to the client may build. Each call after the identity file is read writes one `job.run` record through `AuditRouter` (B-42) to the client's own chain, dry run, execute or refused. It carries the manifest hash (grouped in eight blocks of eight hex digits, as in B-49), counts and `as_of`, never rows. A pack names no other client.

## Acceptance

- **B52-1** for a dental client, findings and controls are mapped to 45 CFR 164.308/312 items; for a financial client, to 16 CFR 314.4 elements; every mapping line cites the governance map id (`BA-n`, `GLBA-n`)
- **B52-2** a BAA and vendor due-diligence tracker lists each vendor, data classes, contract status and review date (synthetic rows)
- **B52-3** every page is labelled "mapping for the client's compliance owner; not legal advice"
- **B52-4** for an investment-adviser client, findings map to SEC Regulation S-P as amended in 2024 (incident response program, service-provider oversight, customer notice), each mapping marked hedged until checked against the rule text; the HIPAA pack stays for any healthcare client (D6-12)

## Known limits (list of what is unverified)

- **Not legal advice, not a certification.** Nothing here says a client meets HIPAA, the Safeguards Rule or Regulation S-P. Which items an assessor or regulator would want, and whether a regime applies to a client, are for the compliance owner and counsel.
- **Citations are UNVERIFIED.** They are copied from `docs/map/05-governance.md`, which was written from memory; none was checked against the eCFR or the Federal Register. Regulation S-P (17 CFR 248.30 paragraph numbers, scope, compliance dates) is the least certain and is hedged line by line. The HHS Security Rule overhaul and the exact FTC notification effective date are also noted as unverified in the governance map.
- **Fixture-based.** Findings, vendors, contract and BAA rows, and client regimes are synthetic. The findings are not the output of an assessment; matching by topic is a convenience, not an analysis. No live tenant, contract or BAA is read.
- **The 365-day vendor review interval** is a fixture setting, not a requirement of any rule.
- **The mapping is many-to-many by topic.** A finding can appear under several items; a missing finding means none was supplied, not that the item is met.
- **AI-provider vendors** (hosted models, MCP servers) belong in the AI vendor register (C-43, B-90), not in this tracker's synthetic rows.
- Nothing schedules the run, retains the output for six years, chases a renewal or edits an agreement.
