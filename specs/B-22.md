## Why

The next technician should not have to ask Hannah Ortiz again what already happened. Today the dispatcher types the client, the contact and a summary by hand; an escalation starts with "what did you try?". This item proposes the three intake fields from the client's own PSA contacts, and builds a fact pack in which every line says where it came from.

## Behaviour

- `workflows/summary/` (`python -m workflows.summary ...`).
- **Intake match** (`match_intake`). The sender address is looked up in the contact lists of the clients' PSA adapters (one adapter per client, `B-18`). An exact address match at exactly one client proposes that client and that contact (`how = email`). An address that is a contact at two clients proposes nothing (`ambiguous`). No exact match but a domain that belongs to exactly one client proposes the client and no contact (`domain`). Anything else proposes nothing (`none`). The affected user, when different from the sender, is matched only against the proposed client's contacts; a user who is not one of that client's contacts is reported as `not a contact of this client` and never looked up elsewhere. A match to an inactive contact is proposed and flagged.
- **The model never chooses.** Client and contact come from the lookup above. A summariser (optional hook, a model later) gets the ticket text only and returns one string; the string is length-limited, scanned, and used as the summary and nothing else. The default summariser is deterministic: the subject and the first sentences of the body, quoted.
- **Summary.** At most 400 characters. Each sentence is the subject or a sentence of the body, verbatim.
- **Escalation package** (`build_package`). Built from the proposed client's PSA adapter only, plus that client's tenant for device facts. Sections: ticket facts, steps tried (PSA ticket notes, and sentences in which the requester says what they tried), related tickets (same contact in the previous 30 days; similar title within four hours), device facts (the affected user's registered authenticator device names and whether the account is enabled). Every line is a `Fact(label, value, source)`: `value` is copied from the field named by `source` (`psa:ticket/T-5301#description`, `psa:ticket-note/90001#description`, `psa:contact/40001#title`, `tenant:user/<upn>#authentication.methods[1].displayName`). The builder re-checks every fact against its source before returning and fails closed. Sections with nothing say so; the code never writes a fact of its own.
- **Injection.** A ticket whose text is flagged by `detect_injection` gets the summary `Flagged: ...` (rule ids only), a package with metadata facts only (no free text from the ticket, its notes or related tickets), and no summariser call. Related tickets and notes that are themselves flagged are withheld and counted.
- **Isolation.** Packages read one client's adapter and tenant. A ticket id of another client is `no such ticket for this client`, the same words as an absent one.
- Data: `workflows/summary/data/psa/<client>.json`, PSA-shaped (Companies, Contacts, Tickets, TicketNotes) for the three synthetic clients, generated by `evals/summary/build_psa.py` from the tenant users and the ticket files, plus 12 hand-written synthetic technician notes (`evals/summary/notes.json`). Field names follow `fixtures/psa/SOURCES.md`; status values are placeholders; `affectedUserEmail` is a local field, not an Autotask field (UNVERIFIED).

## Acceptance

- **B22-1** For each ticket the workflow proposes client, contact and a short summary. Client and contact are matched only against that client's PSA contacts and never chosen by the model: a summariser that returns a different client or contact changes nothing; the ticket's own `client` field is ignored; an address that is a contact at two clients is ambiguous and proposes nothing; the affected user is matched only inside the proposed client; the summary is at most 400 characters and made of quoted sentences.
- **B22-2** An escalation package lists ticket facts, steps tried, related tickets and device facts from read levers (read-only adapter calls and the tenant), each fact citing its source field. Nothing is invented: every fact value is a substring of the field it cites, checked in the builder and again in the eval against the raw fixture files. Another client's ticket id, contact, notes or devices never appear in a package.
- **B22-3** Fixture-based eval report (`evals/summary/report.md`, labelled fixture-based) on the 210-ticket gold set: fact recall at least 90% of the labelled summary facts, zero unsupported facts, zero wrong-client matches, 15 of 15 injection tickets summarised as flagged and not obeyed (no lever, no model call, no free text in the package).
