# B-21 Phase 2 ticket set: 210 labelled tickets and a third synthetic client

Status: **built** (fixtures only, checked by `tests/py/fixtures/test_b21_phase2_tickets.py`). No workflow reads these tickets yet; `B-22` to `B-25` and `B-55` are the first consumers.

> Independently built prototype. Not affiliated with any vendor's or real company's systems. Synthetic data only.

Inventory: `W-SD-01` (intake and summary), `W-SD-03` (duplicates and outages), `W-SD-04` (SLA watch), `W-SD-05` (draft reply from the KB), `W-SD-06` (backlog grooming). Controls: `C-45` (synthetic data, honest labels), `C-41` (evaluation gate before promotion: a gold set big enough to measure). Depends on nothing.

## Why

The Phase 1 set (60 tickets, two clients) is enough to show triage working, not to measure summaries, duplicate grouping, SLA risk or cited replies. Phase 2 needs a gold set with those labels, and a third client so that isolation tests compare three tenants instead of two. The new client and most new tickets sit in the verticals the MSP publishes (financial services, RIAs, legal; `docs/map/07-msp-profile.md` MP-40, decision D6-13). Contoso Dental stays as the W1 demo client.

## Behaviour

- `fixtures/tickets/phase2/<client>.json` for `contoso-dental` (50 tickets, `T-5101`..`T-5150`), `fabrikam-capital` (80, `T-5201`..`T-5280`) and `litware-legal` (80, `T-5301`..`T-5380`). One ticket per line so a reviewer can read a diff.
- `fixtures/tenant/litware-legal.json`: the third mock tenant (9 users, 2 PSA tickets, 10 KB articles `KB-LL-001`..`KB-LL-010`), same shape as the other two, loadable by `MockTenant`.
- The schema of a Phase 2 ticket and every label is documented in `evals/phase2/README.md`.
- The Phase 1 files `fixtures/tickets/contoso-dental.json` and `fabrikam-capital.json` are not changed. `workflows.triage.load_tickets()` reads only the top level of `fixtures/tickets/`, so it still returns exactly the 60 Phase 1 tickets and the triage eval report is unchanged.

## Acceptance

- **B21-1** At least 200 synthetic tickets across Contoso Dental, Fabrikam Capital and Litware Legal (210 built), each with gold labels for category, priority, queue, duplicate group, SLA tier, summary facts and reply source articles (plus expected lever and injection, as in Phase 1). Every summary fact is a verbatim substring of the field it cites; every reply source is an article in the same client's KB; every duplicate group has two or more tickets of one client and one category within four hours. The original 60 tickets are untouched (content hash pinned, `load_tickets()` still returns the same 60 ids) and no Phase 2 id collides with them.
- **B21-2** At least 20 tickets carry synthetic PHI- or NPI-shaped strings (23 built: patient names and dates of birth, member ids, investor and client names, account numbers, SSN-shaped strings, 555-01xx phone numbers), each labelled with its kind, class (`phi`, `npi`, `pii`) and exact text. Shapes are fictional on purpose (account numbers start `00-`, SSN-shaped strings use the never-issued `000` area, phones are 555-01xx), and no unlabelled string of those shapes appears in any ticket, so a later leakage eval has a complete answer key.
- **B21-3** Every new file carries `_note` (or `_meta.note`) saying it is synthetic; every ticket file carries an `as_of`, and every `created` and `updated` is a UTC timestamp at or before it (`created` <= `updated`). Labels are never read by the code under test (STD-FX-5): no module in `core/`, `workflows/` or `levers/` names the Phase 2 folder, the Phase 1 loader does not pick it up, and no label value (KB id, duplicate group id, label key) appears in a ticket's subject or body.
- **B21-4** At least half of the new tickets belong to Fabrikam Capital and Litware Legal (160 of 210). Contoso Dental keeps its Phase 1 file and stays the W1 demo client (D6-13). Every requester and affected user belongs to the ticket's own client, except on tickets labelled as injection (cross-client requests are attacks, and are labelled so).
