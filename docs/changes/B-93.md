## Why

New AI tools arrive through a door, not a side window. The checker fails the build when a model, lever namespace, adapter or the MCP server has no approved, in-date, evidenced record, and when the vendor register holds an approved hosted vendor that no approved provider record authorises.

## Individual to macro (L1-L4)

- L1: a technician proposing a new local model fills one record and sees exactly which evidence is missing.
- L2: Contoso Dental's tickets reach a hosted model only after a record names the data classes, retention terms and BAA or ZDR status (worked example, synthetic).
- L3: the MSP has one file that says who approved each model and tool, until when, and on what evidence.
- L4: clients' patients and investors are not exposed to a tool nobody accountable agreed to.

## Honest limits

- Baseline records `AC-001` to `AC-028` describe what existed on 2026-10-27; their reviewer entries are role placeholders, not real sign-off.
- Cited tests are proven to exist by name, not run. Retention terms are not checked against any real contract (UNVERIFIED).
- Namespace and adapter evidence is shared tests, not per-namespace. Risk and data classes are declared, not measured.
- Not a certification, an attestation or legal advice.

## DECISIONS proposal

D-0xx Tool and model approvals are records, checked by code (B-93, 2026-10-27). One JSON file of records keyed by kind and subject; the checker reads config, the lever registry, adapters and the MCP server, so a new thing without an approved in-date record fails. The vendor register is reached only through an approved `provider` record named in `approval_ref`. Why: W-GOV-02; C-28, C-43. Alternative: a prose-only process (rejected: nothing would fail). What would change it: the MSP's real AI change process (D6-17).
