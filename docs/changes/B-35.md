## Why

A new hire can work on day one, and the request is proven to be legitimate. One form becomes one ordered plan (`user.new`, licenses, groups, archive, sign-in pass), refused up front if `contact.check` does not know the requester, with each step shown as a dry-run diff. After the leads execute it, the welcome and the manager hand-off are drafted for a person to send.

## Individual to macro (L1-L4)

- L1: Casey Moreno starts on Monday with a mailbox, the right groups and a way to sign in, and her manager knows what was set up and what to do on the first morning.
- L2: the practice gets a working account for a named person, and only its own authorized contact can ask for one; a stranger using Sam Patel's name is refused before anything is proposed.
- L3: the MSP's desk replaces six admin screens with one plan, two lead approvals and an audit record without any secret; a missing seat or a privileged group shows up as a blocked step with a reason, never as a silent skip or a purchase.
- L4: every client's joiners follow the same checked path; the role bundles are data in one file, so a new client or role is a data change, not new code.

## DECISIONS proposal

D-0xx New accounts come from a form and a role table, and are created without a credential (B-35, 2026-10-22). `user.new` is R3 with two distinct leads and the requester+contact verification; it creates the account enabled with no password, no license and no pass; the first sign-in is the separate `user.new-tap` step. The plan is built from a fixed form (no free text) and `roles.json`, and refuses before any dry-run when `contact.check` says no. Why: C-05, C-06, C-07, C-29, C-35, D6-05, D6-06. Alternatives: one lever that creates everything including the pass (rejected: a secret and a license decision in one R3 call, harder to review); roles in code (rejected: per-client data, not code); registering `user.disable` as the undo (rejected: it does not remove the account). Would change: a real Graph adapter, an `onboarding` queue source, a mailbox-create lever.
