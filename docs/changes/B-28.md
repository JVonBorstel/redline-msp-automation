## Why

An identity change at scale is only as safe as the rules around the approval. After this item the runner, not each lever family, decides how many leads must approve, that they proved who they are, that an irreversible change is approved today and not last week, and that one applied change is not applied again under a different approval. Device and mailbox levers in later waves inherit all of it.

## Individual to macro (L1 to L4)

- **L1, one person.** Priya Nair's MFA reset now needs two signed-in leads, a callback to the number on file and her office manager's confirmation. A caller who knows her date of birth gets nowhere, and an account holding an admin role is never reset on a phone call.
- **L2, the client business.** A client can ask for more approvers than the default (a client record can raise, never lower) and names who may confirm a change on its behalf. A lead scoped to another client cannot approve here, and the per-client lock means one client's slow change does not stall another's.
- **L3, the MSP.** One engineer cannot approve and execute alone, and an approver is a person who signed in, with the method and session recorded (a demo pick is marked `demo`). Every record carries `request_id`, `actor_kind` and `lever_version`, so an auditor can follow one request and tell what an agent did from what a person did.
- **L4, community.** Help-desk impersonation is the common way into a managed environment. Two humans, a callback and a second confirmation, applied identically at every client, make the weak client harder to use as a stepping stone.

Each acceptance id has tests that run the real runner offline on a private copy of the mock tenant, check the tenant bytes are unchanged on every denial, and check the audit record (status, reason, fields). ## Not done, and caveats

- When this item merged, no shipped path ran under a policy: the queue and the page sent one unsigned approval. **Since closed** by `docs/changes/B-28-dual-approval.md` (D-067): `python -m ui` runs under a policy and the queue collects a second, different lead. The offline CLIs still run without one (W1).
- "The approval page shows `no undo`": the page already prints `NO_UNDO` (`ui/app.py`); the runner exports `undo_notice`. No page edit was made.
- The replay guard is literal. After an applied key, a different approval for the same key is denied for good, including a legitimate repeat later. See the proposal below.
- Directory roles are read from `directoryRoles` in the tenant state (Graph `directoryRoles?$expand=members` shape, UNVERIFIED against a live tenant). The W2 mock tenant files have none, so the check is exercised with a test-added fixture. Any role counts as privileged.
- The replay guard scans the audit log on each execute.

## DECISIONS proposal

D-0xx Approval rules live in the runner and turn on with a policy (B-28, W3, 2026-10-21). Two signed-in leads for R3 (one for R2), minimums raised per lever or client and never lowered, irreversible levers need approvals under 24 hours old, and a key that an earlier, different approval already applied is denied (STD-LV-6a, literal). `requester+contact` levers need a callback record naming the number's source plus a confirmation from a contact on the client record, and refuse a target holding a directory role. Why: D6-05 and D6-06; one place to enforce them keeps later lever families from editing the runner. Alternative: default-on, which breaks the single-approval queue and page until B-65 lands. What would change it: B-65 wiring (then default the policy on), or evidence that the literal replay guard blocks legitimate repeats (then key the guard on a change id or add an expiry).
