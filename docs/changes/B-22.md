# B-22 change note: ticket summaries, intake matching and escalation context packages

Status: **built** (workflow, CLI, eval, tests). Read-only: nothing is saved to a PSA, nothing is sent (HO-9). Synthetic data only; all numbers are fixture-based.

## What changed

- `workflows/summary/` (`common.py`, `intake.py`, `package.py`, CLI `python -m workflows.summary`, README).
  - Intake proposal: client and contact are looked up from the sender address in each client's PSA adapter contacts (B-18), never chosen by a model; the affected user is matched only inside the proposed client; an address at two clients is `ambiguous` and proposes nothing; the ticket's own `client` field is ignored. Summary: subject plus up to three quoted body sentences, at most 400 characters; an optional summariser hook can only change the summary text and falls back on bad output.
  - Escalation package: ticket facts, steps tried (PSA notes, requester-stated steps), related tickets (same contact in 30 days, similar wording in 4 hours), device facts (registered sign-in devices, account enabled). Every fact is `Fact(label, value, source)` and is traced to its source field before the package is returned.
  - Injection: a flagged ticket is summarised as `Flagged: ...`, never reaches the summariser, and gets a metadata-only package; flagged notes and related tickets are withheld and counted.
- `workflows/summary/data/psa/<client>.json`: PSA-shaped synthetic data for the three clients (generated, 210 tickets, 24 contacts, 12 notes).
- `evals/summary/` (`build_psa.py`, `notes.json`, `gold.py`, `report.py`, `report.md`): the generator and the eval. Facts are traced against data rebuilt from the raw ticket, tenant and note files, not against what the workflow loaded.
- `specs/B-22.md`: acceptance B22-1 to B22-3 (refined). `tests/py/summary/test_b22_summary.py`: 34 tests named `test_b22_<k>_...`.

**Outside `owns`:** none. The generated PSA JSON lives under `workflows/summary/data/` (owned folder).

## Why

A technician who gets an escalation should not have to ask Hannah Ortiz again what happened, and a dispatcher should not retype client, contact and summary. Looking the client up in the PSA contact list (instead of letting text or a model decide) keeps one client's ticket from being filed under another, and a fact pack whose every line cites a field cannot carry an invented step.

## Individual to macro (L1-L4)

- L1: Hannah Ortiz (synthetic paralegal, Litware Legal) reports a dead Authenticator before a 5:00 pm filing (T-5301); the next technician sees her ticket, her role, her registered iPhone and that the account is enabled, with sources, and does not call her to ask.
- L2: Litware Legal and Fabrikam Capital hold client matters and investor accounts; a ticket from a Litware address cannot be proposed for Fabrikam, a cross-client "affected user" is shown as not a contact, and a spoofed instruction in a ticket (15 of 15 in the set) is flagged and left out of the package instead of being repeated to a technician or a model.
- L3: the MSP gets a proposal and a fact pack for every ticket (210 of 210 matched and traced, 0 unsupported facts), less re-asking and re-typing at hand-off, and an eval that must pass before a prompt like `summarize.v1` can be promoted (C-41, still partial: no model is scored here).
- L4: the pattern (look up identity, quote and cite, withhold what looks like an attack, publish the limits) is generic and carries no real client data, so other small MSPs can reuse it.

## DECISIONS note proposal

D-0xx Intake proposals and escalation packages are lookups and quotations (B-22, 2026-10-21). The client and contact of a ticket are proposed only by matching the sender address against each client's own PSA contact list (one adapter per client); a model, the ticket text and the ticket's own `client` field never choose them, and an address that is a contact at two clients proposes nothing. The escalation package is made only of `Fact(label, value, source)` lines whose value is copied from the cited field and re-checked before return; sections with nothing say so. A ticket flagged by the injection detector is summarised as flagged and contributes no free text. Why: closes the wrong-client and invented-step failure modes (C-12, C-13, C-27) at the code level instead of by prompt wording. Alternatives: a model proposes client and contact (rejected: untrusted text would choose a tenant); a model writes the package (rejected until an eval can show zero unsupported facts for model output; the trace check is ready for it). What would change it: a model summariser that passes this eval plus the injection set, or a real Autotask binding with different contact semantics (shared contacts, aliases).

Proposed registry follow-up (not done here; `levers/registry.json` and `levers/registry.d/` are not owned by B-22): read levers `ticket.related`, `user.devices` (and later `device.get` once an RMM adapter exists), so device and related-ticket facts go through the lever runner and its audit log like `kb.search` does. Until then the workflow reads the PSA adapter and tenant directly (read-only) and writes no audit record.

## Honest limits

- Fixture-based: tickets, contacts and notes are synthetic, tidy and written by one author; 12 hand-written notes supply most of the "steps tried"; real tickets are messier.
- Recall is 100% largely because every description sentence is quoted; precision and technician usefulness are not measured. Related-ticket rules are crude (193 of 210 packages list one or more).
- PSA field names follow `fixtures/psa/SOURCES.md`; status codes and `affectedUserEmail` are UNVERIFIED local choices. The MSP's actual stack (Autotask, Datto RMM, IT Glue) is not connected; RMM and IT Glue facts are absent.
- No audit record is written for the reads (adapter calls, not lever calls), and package text can contain PHI/NPI-shaped strings from the ticket; both belong to the lever and redaction work above.
