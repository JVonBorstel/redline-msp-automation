## Why

Documentation and runbooks drift. A page that lists a retired server, omits its replacement, or names a contact who left misleads the technician; a runbook with no owner, no review date, a dead link or a lever that is not registered fails when it is needed. This finds the drift and proposes the fix.

## Individual to macro (L1-L4)

- L1: Ana, a technician at Contoso Dental, sees that the imaging page calls the dental server a desktop and that a hygienist who left is still listed, instead of finding out mid-incident.
- L2: each client's report names only that client's devices, contacts and licences; the other site's decoy RMM devices never appear.
- L3: the MSP gets a per-client list of stale, missing and contradictory pages, and a runbook lint that exits non-zero while a runbook has no owner or review date.
- L4: fewer wrong-instruction outages at small practices and firms; the runbooks an auditor reads name an owner and a recent review.

## Honest limits

- The five committed runbook files fail the linter today (no owner or last-reviewed line; several lack sections). They are listed in `specs/B-59.md` and not edited here; the test requires the list to match the linter's output.
