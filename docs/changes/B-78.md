## Why

"Route rules first, small model next, strong model only where needed, thresholds set by eval results" was a design statement with no measurement behind it. This gives a repeatable run that scores the three decider kinds on the gold tickets, reports accuracy and escalation per category, and writes proposed escalation thresholds together with the run that produced them.

## Individual to macro (L1-L4)

- L1: Ana, a technician, gets a quick answer on routine tickets from the cheap tiers and a person-checked result when the cheap tiers are unsure.
- L2: Contoso Dental's tickets stay on the cheapest tier that is reliably right for their category; hard ones escalate.
- L3: the MSP can show why each category escalates at the confidence it does, and what the cascade costs against always using the strong model.
- L4: smaller, local-first models do more of the work, so less data leaves the building and less compute is spent.

All of that is the intent. The numbers behind it are from mocks (below).

## Result on the fixtures (mock tiers)

From `evals/routing/report.md` at the committed run: rules 78.3%, small 95.0%, strong 95.0% category accuracy; the derived-threshold cascade 98.3% in-sample and 100% on the 30-ticket holdout, at 249 relative cost units against 1200 for strong on everything. These come from hand-written keyword mocks and say nothing about real models.

## Honest limits and unverified

- The three tiers are keyword mocks with confidences declared by the author, who also wrote the tickets. Thresholds are a mechanical function of that fixture. UNVERIFIED: that any real model's confidence is calibrated enough for such thresholds.
- No local model was run and no model client is imported, so B78-1's local-model half is **not built**. The report row reads `skipped: no local model`; there is no code path that would fill it.
- Latency is not measured. Cost is an assumed relative unit (0, 1, 20), not money.
- 60 tickets, 4 to 9 per category: several categories end up `null` (always escalate) because of the minimum-support rule. The holdout is 30 tickets. In-sample numbers are optimistic.
- The cascade out-scoring the strong tier is an artifact of the mock strong tier being wrong on a few tickets.
- Only the classify task is scored; summarize and reason have no gold set. Injection tickets are scored as plain tickets.
- Nothing consumes the proposal and `config/routing.json` has no threshold field. Applying thresholds, and the gateway side of it, is for the reviewer and B-80.
- `ruff format --check .` reports 2 files that need reformatting in the base branch (for example `tests/py/tools/test_orphan_tests.py`); they are not part of this change and were left alone.

## DECISIONS proposal

D-0xx Escalation thresholds are derived, not chosen (B-78, 2026-10-27). For each cheaper tier and predicted category, the threshold is the lowest declared confidence whose accepted decisions are at least 95% correct with at least 3 of them; otherwise `null` (always escalate). The result is written to `evals/routing/routing.proposed.json` with the run's inputs hashed, marked `proposed` and `applied: false`, and is never applied automatically. The tiers are mocks until a local model exists; the report says `skipped: no local model` rather than a number. Why: C-16, C-41, C-15; docs/BLUEPRINT.md section 3. Alternative: hand-set thresholds (rejected: opinion). What would change it: a local model run on the gold set, a larger labelled set, or a different target accuracy per risk class.
